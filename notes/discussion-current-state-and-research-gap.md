# The CCS Virtual Office Dashboard: Current State and Research Gap

The CCS Virtual Office Dashboard is a live, browser-based system that gives the
College of Computer Studies a single point of reference for its virtual
help-desk operations. It draws directly from the Google Sheets that staff use
to log every client interaction, so the figures on screen always reflect the
latest recorded activity rather than a static, periodically-issued report. In
its present form, the dashboard already covers most of the descriptive ground
an administrator would need day to day: an Operations Dashboard that tracks
every logged concern by status (Resolved, Transferred, In Progress) and
automatically classifies it into a topic — enrollment, thesis and capstone,
practicum, grades, fees, and so on — an audit log that can be filtered,
searched, and exported for closer inspection; an Evaluation Analytics page
that summarizes client satisfaction survey responses into service-quality
scores and a sample of real client feedback; and, most recently, a Knowledge
Base that repackages resolved and transferred concerns into searchable
precedents so a staff member can look up how a similar issue was handled
before. The workflow built around these tools is manual by design: an
administrator logs in, chooses a month to review, reads through the resulting
stat tiles, charts, and tables, and forms their own conclusions about what the
numbers mean before acting on them or relaying them upward.

This workflow is where the dashboard's current limitations surface. The
system is descriptive, not interpretive — it can show that transferred
concerns spiked in a given month or that satisfaction dipped in one service
dimension, but turning that observation into a decision still falls entirely
on whoever is reading the screen. Preparing anything beyond the built-in
views, such as a monthly report for higher administration, means manually
cross-referencing multiple tabs and re-typing figures into a separate
document, which is slow and repetitive. The "insights" the dashboard does
surface are generated from fixed, keyword-based rules rather than any
adaptive analysis of the underlying data, so they cannot flag an emerging
pattern the rules were not written to anticipate. As a result, the quality of
whatever decision comes out of the dashboard depends heavily on the
experience of the particular administrator using it — a newer staff member
and a veteran one, looking at the same numbers, may walk away with very
different read on what needs attention. These gaps point to a clear
opportunity: rather than remain a well-organized display of historical data,
the CCS Virtual Office Dashboard could be extended into an intelligent
decision support system — one that interprets its own data, drafts its own
summaries, and surfaces recommendations, reducing how much of the analytical
burden still rests on the administrator.
